# Trademark Policy

This policy covers PatchFrog's brand identity, separately from the source
code license in [`LICENSE`](LICENSE) (see [`docs/licensing.md`](docs/licensing.md)
for how the two relate). It is a concise statement of product/brand intent,
not a substitute for legal advice, and does not create or waive any legal
right.

## What this policy covers

- The **"PatchFrog"** name.
- The **PatchFrog logo** and **official frog icon**
  (`docs/assets/brand/patchfrog-logo.png`, `patchfrog-icon.png` -- see
  [`docs/brand.md`](docs/brand.md)).
- The **`patchfrog[bot]`** GitHub identity.
- The phrase **"Official PatchFrog"**.
- **"PatchFrog Cloud"**.

## Allowed without separate permission

- Truthful, non-misleading references, e.g. "compatible with PatchFrog" or
  "built on PatchFrog".
- Describing a self-hosted deployment or fork as **"based on PatchFrog"**,
  where doing so is accurate and not misleading about its official status.
- Factual references in documentation, blog posts, comparisons, or
  academic/technical writing.

## Not allowed without permission

- Implying Anthropic-, Google-, GitHub-, or PatchFrog-project endorsement
  of a fork, deployment, or unrelated product.
- Presenting a fork or self-hosted deployment as **the official PatchFrog
  service** or as **PatchFrog Cloud**.
- Using "PatchFrog" branding (name, logo, or frog icon) as the **primary
  identity** of a competing fork or hosted service -- i.e., naming or
  branding a separately-operated hosted offering so that it reads as *the*
  PatchFrog product rather than a derivative.
- Impersonating the `patchfrog[bot]` GitHub identity, or configuring a
  self-hosted deployment's bot account/App to present itself as
  `patchfrog[bot]` or as visually/behaviorally indistinguishable from it.
- Using the official logo or frog icon in a way that suggests an unofficial
  deployment is operated by the PatchFrog project.

## Self-hosted deployments

Running your own self-hosted instance under [`LICENSE`](LICENSE) is
expected and welcome (see [`docs/product-boundary.md`](docs/product-boundary.md)).
This policy does not restrict *running* PatchFrog -- it restricts how the
**PatchFrog name/brand/bot identity** may be presented in connection with a
deployment you operate. A self-hosted instance should identify itself under
its own GitHub App identity, not `patchfrog[bot]` (see
[`docs/product-boundary.md`](docs/product-boundary.md#github-app-boundary)).

## Scope and certainty

This document intentionally does not overstate legal certainty. It states
PatchFrog's current position and intent plainly; it is not a claim that
every scenario above is settled trademark law, and it is not legal advice.
If you're unsure whether a specific use is fine, ask first.
